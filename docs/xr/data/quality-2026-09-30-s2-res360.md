# Still grading, 2026-09-30 s2: r480 (640x480) vs r360 (640x360 row-crop), 167 fps, 2 Mbit/s

Stills: `scripts/quest/out/quality_private/s2-2026-09-30/` (private, not committed): `full-r480.png`, `full-r360.png`
(compositor screencaps 3664x1920, both eyes) and `quality-2026-09-30-s2-r{480,360}-{1,2}.jpg` (left-eye crops, 1253x1171).
Graded by a subagent against the fixed rubric (artefacts / sharpness / noise / exposure, 1–10). No people are described.

> **Note 2026-09-30 (pixelpilot-xr-25):** the air frames are centred (coordinator: IMX415 window registers and a cross-correlation of the r360 frame in the r480 frame peak at 60 rows), so the top-heavy crop seen here is in the Quest rendering path; the bottom streaks are the 8 padding rows of the 368-row coded frame the app draws ([link-envelope S2 section](../link-envelope.md)).

## Method

- Viewed every still at native brightness, then with gain (x6–x40, gamma 0.6) because the scene is almost black.
  Only the boosted views show the texture. Helper scripts and resized copies are in the session scratchpad, not the repo.
- Layer geometry: per-column and per-row edges of the non-black area in the left eye of each full screencap.
- Scale and FOV: the r360 picture was cut into horizontal bands and each band was matched by correlation
  against r480 (shift search ±200 px, and a global scale search sy 0.80–1.24, sx 0.96–1.04). This compares
  content, so where the layer sits on screen does not matter.
- Padding: zoom on the r360 bottom edge, plus a ratio of vertical to horizontal pixel differences measured
  inward from the bottom edge.
- Every screencap pixel coordinate below is in the 3664x1920 full frame, left eye (x < 1832). The compositor's
  lens pre-distortion curves the layer edges (barrel), so all pixel extents are approximate.

## Scores (1–10, higher is better)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| r480-1 | 5 | 3 | 5 | 2 | 3 |
| r480-2 | 5 | 3 | 5 | 2 | 3 |

| r360-2 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 3 |

- **Artefacts.** No frozen, torn or corrupted areas in any still, and no macroblock bands from loss. With gain, both
  arms show coarse blocking over the whole layer (flat 8–16 px blocks and posterised colour steps), which is typical
  of very low bits per pixel on a dark, noisy source. At normal brightness this is barely visible. r360 scores one
  point lower for two reasons. Its dark areas are lifted a little above black (see exposure), so they show as
  mottled blocks where r480 shows flat black. It also has the smeared strip at the bottom edge (see the padding
  section).
- **Sharpness.** Both are very soft: the light sources are blurred blobs, with no edges or fine detail to judge.
  High-frequency energy inside the layer is the same in both arms (0.219–0.230 for all four crops).
- **Noise.** Mostly crushed into blocks. r360 shows more visible mottling in the shadows.
- **Exposure.** Mean luma inside the layer is 4.8–5.5/255 for r480 and 5.9–6.1 for r360, with a maximum of about
  28/255 everywhere. Both are badly under-exposed. r360 is about 10–25 % brighter, probably because auto-exposure
  no longer sees the brighter top band that the crop removes [INFERRED].

## r480 vs r360 per criterion

| Criterion | Better | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Artefacts | r480 (slightly) | Its shadows stay flat black instead of mottled blocks, and it has no bottom smear strip |
| Sharpness | tie | Both are blurred blobs; high-frequency energy is equal |
| Noise | r480 (slightly) | Its shadows are crushed rather than mottled |
| Exposure | r360 (slightly) | 10–25 % higher mean luma, caused by auto-exposure rather than by the mode |
| Decode time | r360 | 1.34 vs 1.47 ms |

**Bits per pixel.** At the rates the overlay reports, r480 gets 2.2e6 / (640·480·167) = 0.043 bpp and r360 gets
2.0e6 / (640·360·167) = 0.052 bpp, about 21 % more (33 % more if both are at 2.0 Mbit/s). **It does not show.**
Block size, posterisation and high-frequency energy look the same. At about 0.05 bpp on a near-black scene, both
arms sit in the same starved regime, and 20–30 % more bits per pixel is below what this scene can reveal
[INFERRED: equal hf-energy + visual comparison]. A bright, textured scene is needed to test it.

## Field of view (the main decision)

- **Width is unchanged.** Both layers are about 1220 px wide in the screencap (x ≈ 240–1461), and content matches at
  horizontal scale sx = 1.00 [PROVEN: band correlation 0.88–0.96 in the central bands].
- **Height is reduced.** The r480 layer runs from y ≈ 431 at the top centre to about 1320 at the bottom centre
  (about 890 px; the bottom edge is estimated, because r480's lower-left scene is pure black). The r360 layer runs
  from y ≈ 534 to about 1290 (about 756 px, **~85 % of the r480 height**). The 16:9 layer is shorter at the same
  width: it looks smaller, not letterboxed (there are no bars; the area above and below is just the dark void) and
  not stretched. The stats overlay moves up with the layer's bottom edge.
- **No distortion.** The best content match is sx = 1.00, sy ≈ 0.95–0.96. r360 content is about 4–5 % taller than
  the same content in r480, which is within the error of the barrel-distorted band fit. A 16:9 picture stretched to
  a 4:3 layer would be +33 % taller, and the reverse would be −25 %. Neither is present, so the aspect ratio is
  preserved [PROVEN: global scale search over sy 0.80–1.24].
- **Visible scene fraction.** Nominally 360/480 = **75 % of the r480 scene** (100 % of the width × 75 % of the
  height). Measured in screen space, the r360 content covers about 80 % of the r480 layer height (r480 y ≈ 556–1265
  out of 431–1320). The edge rows of r480 are compressed by the lens pre-distortion, so this agrees with 75 %
  [INFERRED].
- **The loss looks top-heavy, not symmetric.** A landmark on the left edge (a small magenta patch) sits at
  y ≈ 665–720 in both arms. The bottom of the blue light cone (y ≈ 1280) lines up with r360's bottom edge.
  Measured against the r480 layer, r360 loses about **105–125 px at the top and about 30–40 px at the bottom**
  (about 3:1). A centred crop would lose 60 rows at each end [INFERRED: band shifts dy = +4…+26 px; the r480 bottom
  edge is uncertain because its lower-left scene is pure black, and if that edge is actually near 1285, the bottom
  loss is close to 0]. **Check this with a test chart or a bright scene**: the sensor window may start around row
  ~80–90 rather than 60, or the r480 mode itself may be offset.
- **In practice** the r360 view drops the top band of the scene (the brighter upper wall area in this room) plus a
  thin bottom strip, and shows everything else at the same angular size.

## Padding check (368 coded rows vs 360)

- **No garbage band.** No green, grey or random lines at the r360 bottom edge.
- **Probably not cleanly cropped.** The bottom ~15–25 px of the r360 layer (about 8–12 source rows at ~2 px/row)
  are **vertical streaks**: each column keeps one colour down to the edge. That is the signature of the H.264
  encoder's edge-replicated padding rows (rows 360–367 copy row 359) being shown. At the same place in r480, the
  blue light cone ends in a rounded shape instead. The vertical/horizontal difference ratio dips to 0.4–0.8 in the
  bottom 6–22 px against about 1.0 further up, which is a weak signal (8-bit values of ~10/255). The overlay reports
  `640x368`, so the app reads the coded size.
- **Verdict:** the SPS crop is **most likely not applied** to the displayed picture. The 8 replicated padding rows
  are drawn as a thin smeared strip at the bottom, which is harmless-looking but not clean [INFERRED]. To confirm,
  log MediaFormat `crop-bottom` / `crop-top` in the decoder output format and look at a bright scene.

## Crops (quality_*.jpg)

- The r480 crops hold the whole layer and the overlay. The layer's left edge is at, or a few px past, the crop's
  left border. That border is the same in both arms, so it does not affect the comparison.
- The r360 crops were cut with the old box, so they include about 150 px of empty black above the 16:9 layer and
  empty black below the overlay. The layer is fully inside the box, with nothing cut off.

## Limits

- **Dark room:** mean luma is about 5–6/255 inside the layer. Sharpness, noise and the bits-per-pixel effect
  **cannot be judged**. Artefact grades rely on x6–x40 gain, which exaggerates blocking the eye would hardly see in
  the headset.
- **Static scene:** there is no motion, so smear, ghosting and motion blocking cannot be judged.
- Auto-exposure differs between the arms and confounds exposure, noise and shadow blocking.
- Only 2 stills per arm, taken a few seconds apart, in one scene. Screencaps are compositor output (lens
  pre-distortion and eye-buffer resampling), so geometry is approximate to a few percent.
- The r480 bottom edge is uncertain (black scene content), which limits how precisely the top/bottom split of the
  FOV loss can be measured.
